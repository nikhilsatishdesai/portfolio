01 · HEADLINE PROJECT

One system, two brands

A production pipeline that takes an episode from topic to upload kit, used for two channels that look and sound nothing alike.

Role: designer, director, operatorOct–Nov 2026Claude Code · ComfyUI · ElevenLabs API · Qwen3-TTS · IndexTTS-2 · Whisper · Stable Audio 3 · DaVinci Resolve Studio

The brief I set myself

Publish a 9–13 minute video, 7–9 Reels and a full packaging set every week for two channels, alone, without the quality dropping. Most of each episode had to be repeatable. The judgement calls had to stay with a person.

What I built

  • A script format the machine can read. Every line has an ID, a speaker, an emotion tag and a visual cue (C05 · KAI · (sceptical) · Wait. Says who?). The voice, shot list, subtitles and Reels are all built from it.
  • One stage order, two tool stacks. The stages and checks are the same for both brands. The voice and picture tools change with the brand (table below).
  • A shot list timed to the voice. Shots are cued to words, not seconds (@C05:insurance), so a re-recorded line doesn't break the edit.
  • A packaging stage. Three thumbnails for YouTube's Test & Compare, Reel covers, an Instagram story and an upload kit with the schedule, description, sources and captions.

What good looks like, and how it's checked

  • Whisper transcribes every voice line and flags any that don't match the script.
  • Contact sheets of every shot and Reel are checked for text overlaps, safe areas and blank frames before anything renders at 4K.
  • A frame validator checks every rendered file. The mix is finished by hand in Resolve to about −14 LUFS.
  • Nothing moves on until I sign off the stage before it.

What I did myself

I designed the stages, wrote every brief and brand rule, set the quality bar and reviewed every output at every gate. I write and fact-check the scripts, clean the voice tracks and do the final edit and mix in DaVinci Resolve. Claude Code wrote the pipeline code (about 54,000 lines of Python) to my specs.

01+ · IN COMFYUI

The ComfyUI workflows, opened up

These are the real graphs my production scripts send to ComfyUI: the same models, prompts and settings. Here they're opened in the editor and run on my own laptop (RTX 4070, 8 GB).

Run 18 Nov 2026ComfyUI 0.45 · Z-Image Turbo · Stable Audio 3 · Qwen3-TTS · IndexTTS-2No paid credits

Two ways to run the same workflow

An artist designs and tweaks a workflow in the editor. When an episode needs 70 backdrops or 90 voice lines, a Python script sends the same graph to ComfyUI once per item, saves every output with its settings and checks the results. I work both ways: the editor to find the look, scripts to make it at scale.

1 · Backdrop plates: Z-Image Turbo, then the pixel engine

Each Republic of Ideas backdrop is painted by Z-Image from a scene description plus a fixed house style, at 1536×640, in 8 steps, about 10–30 seconds on this laptop. A pixel engine then snaps it to the channel's 1280 grid and a fixed 96-colour palette, so every plate looks like it belongs to the same world.

New plates made for this page, with the same workflow and house style. Only the scene description changes.

2 · Music: Stable Audio 3

Every cue for The Jackal's Counsel is written in D minor at 84 BPM, so cues from different episodes cut together. The negative prompt keeps out vocals, mud and clipping.

3 · Designing a voice from a written brief: Qwen3-TTS

No voice actor and no cloned real voice. The character is described in words; I generate three candidates per character and pick by ear.

4 · Performing a line with emotion: IndexTTS-2

The approved voice reads each script line with a written emotion direction (here: "amused, intimate, sly storyteller"), so the same character can whisper, gloat or plead.

5 · A custom node: ElevenLabs with your own account

Two nodes, ElevenLabs Speech and ElevenLabs Music, that sit in the same graphs as the local models. The API key never goes into a workflow file: it's encrypted with Windows' own credential protection. Every take is cached under a fingerprint of its settings, so re-running a workflow never pays twice for the same line, and each file is saved with the exact request that made it.

An explainer series on the narrow places that run the world, hosted by two pixel-art AI characters: Ira, the researcher, and Kai, the sceptic.

Role: writer, director, editorYouTube + InstagramElevenLabs v4 API · ComfyUI (Z-Image) · Stable Audio 3 · Resolve

The challenge

Make geopolitics watchable without a face on camera. The answer was two hosts who argue: Kai interrupts every 30–40 seconds ("Wait. Says who?"), which keeps viewers watching and makes Ira show her sources. The channel's bio says it plainly: AI hosts, every script written and fact-checked by a person.

Episode 2: "China's 2.7 km Nightmare" (Malacca)

  • 9:25, 92 shots, about 90 voice lines, 7 Reels, all in 4K.
  • A thousand years of history in one argument: an iron chain, Zheng He's depot, Portugal's "hand on the throat of Venice", and a stolen ship that gave us the law of the sea.

Brand rules the pipeline enforces

  • Real maps with real geography. Ships stay on water.

  • Five typefaces, each with one job (Pixelify Sans for headlines, VT323 for subtitles, and so on).
  • On Reels the bottom 22% stays clear of the app's buttons.

03

The Jackal's Counsel

1,700-year-old Indian stories (the Panchatantra, Vikram and Betaal) retold as counsel for modern life, in a world of polished stone inlay.

Role: writer, art director, director, editorYouTube + InstagramQwen3-TTS · IndexTTS-2 · Whisper · Stable Audio 3 · code-drawn art · Resolve

The challenge

Give a second brand its own look and voice without building a second pipeline, and do it without paid generation. The look is parchin kari, Mughal stone inlay: every character is assembled from separate "stones" drawn in code, so a jackal can lean in, crack or fall apart on cue. No image in the brand is AI-painted.

What changed from Republic of Ideas

  • Voices: designed from written character briefs with Qwen3-TTS (three candidates per character, picked by ear), then performed with IndexTTS-2, one emotion direction per line.
  • Music: every cue in D minor at 84 BPM, so the trailer and all episodes sound like one channel. Each story gets one signature sound (the drum in "The Hollow Drum", the broom in "The Man with the Broom").
  • Thumbnails: I wrote a thumbnail playbook after studying YouTube Studio data and comparable channels. It changed the rules: one idea, one big face with one emotion, the channel's lapis-blue ground.

Results

In its first month: 11.3K YouTube views and 23.9K Instagram views, almost all from people who don't follow the channel yet. Three Shorts passed 1,000 views each, and long-form viewers stay an average of 3 minutes.

04

The packaging kit

The last stage of the pipeline: everything an episode needs to be found, generated from the same assets and checked like the video.

Role: designer, strategistThumbnails · Reel covers · stories · carousels · upload kits

What one episode ships with

  • Three thumbnails, each with a different news angle, for YouTube's Test & Compare. For Malacca: "China's weak spot", "USA vs China: the oil gap" and "India's island".
  • A cover for every Reel, in the same type treatment as the thumbnails, over a bright frame from that Reel.
  • An Instagram story with space left for the link sticker.
  • An upload kit: a release schedule (two Reels before the long-form, then one a day), a title with two backups if CTR is under 3% after 1,000 impressions, a description with chapters and sources, tags, cards, a pinned comment and every Reel caption.

Why it's a stage, not an afterthought

On The Jackal's Counsel, Shorts get about 10% click-through and long-form about 2.5%. The stories hold people once they click; the packaging is what loses them. So packaging gets its own sign-off gate and its own written rules, like the thumbnail playbook.

06

The Fourth Knock

A short goofy-noir murder mystery that plays in the browser, creative-directed through an AI production pipeline.

Role: creative director, AI production directorStory · game design · 3D · animation · 98 voice lines · original score

The question underneath

Can AI systems be directed like a multidisciplinary team instead of being used as separate generators? One creative direction had to survive story, mystery design, mechanics, illustration, 3D, animation, voice, music and code, across handoffs, failed approaches and pivots, and arrive as a game you can finish in about 18 minutes.

A decision that shows how I direct

The locked-room trick first relied on a cord pulled under a door. The AI answered with a physics simulation: 619 cord segments, 128 solver iterations, and it still failed its own accuracy check. I rejected it for an ordinary spring night latch the player works in three steps. Use the cheapest reliable system that gives the player the experience you want.

HOW I WORK

From a repeat request to a system that runs

I've worked agency-side and in-house. What carries across is one habit: when something gets made twice, I build the system and write down how to run it.

Scope

What repeats, what must stay a human call, and what "good" looks like, written down first.

Build

The cheapest reliable tool for each stage: an API, an open model, ComfyUI, code or a person.

Check

A sign-off gate after every stage, plus automatic checks (transcripts, contact sheets, frame validation).

Document

A bible and a handover guide, kept current with a changelog.

Hand over

Written for the next person, or the next AI assistant, to run without me.
